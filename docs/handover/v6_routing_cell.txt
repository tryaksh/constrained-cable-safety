2. HOW TO WORK ACROSS A LONG SESSION
=======================================================================
The dominant failure mode for a session this long is not a hard bug. It is
drift: by hour six the working context is mostly stale detail, and the session
starts re-deriving what it already settled or forgetting a constraint it agreed
to in hour one. Defend mechanically, not by remembering harder.

  WRITE THE DONE-CONDITION TO DISK BEFORE YOU START EACH STAGE. Each stage below
    names a file under artifacts/cell/. Create it with the acceptance criteria
    BEFORE doing the work, then fill in the measured values. A stage is finished
    when its file says so. This is the one habit that survives a context reset.
  KEEP ONE APPEND-ONLY LOG: artifacts/cell/PROGRESS.md, one line per decision
    with the number that drove it. Never rewrite earlier lines.
  CHECKPOINT BY COMMITTING at the end of every stage, even an ugly one.
  VERIFY AT EACH STAGE, NOT AT THE END. Every stage has a check that runs in
    under a minute. A pipeline wrong at stage 2 and caught at stage 5 costs the
    session.
  SUSPECT THE INSTRUMENT FIRST. This project has now been bitten three times by
    a measurement whose apparatus was wrong: a bending-damping control scaled
    backwards, a video capture that consumed the wrong number of RNG draws, and
    a collision probe built without the project's contact defaults (section 4).
    Every one looked like a finding. Before you believe a surprising number, ask
    what would have to be true about the apparatus for it to be wrong.
  DO NOT POLL LONG RUNS. Launch, then do the next independent thing. You are
    notified when a background command finishes.

THE PERCEPTION RESULT. 16,080 pre-registered requests, 1.49 billion integration
steps, 9.05 hours, zero guard violations, every label independently re-derived
from the raw ledgers with zero disagreements. A single fitted scalar - the
straight-line boot-to-anchor distance at the endpoint a move would reach,
carrying a margin sized from the estimator's own declared error - is not beaten
by more than the registered margin by ANY richer representation, at ANY level of
estimation error, on ANY of three constraint shapes. On clip retention the
network over the whole 24-vertex centreline is significantly WORSE (bootstrap
gap 0.074, interval excluding zero, tight across seeds).

THE COMPOSITION RESULT, which is what the routing task builds on. 720 registered
sequences, 2,160 decisions, 72.9 million steps, zero guard events:
  - The filter earns its place and it is not close. Every one of the 240
    unfiltered sequences lost the required clip - rate 1.000 at all three error
    levels - against 0.400 / 0.569 / 0.545 filtered.
  - The clip budget does NOT compose. Its per-step violation rate rises 0.089
    from the first decision to the last at 2 mm error, past the 0.05 margin.
    Curvature and anchor load DO compose. That is the reverse of the registered
    prediction and the useful direction: the constraint the filter measures
    directly is the one that decays along a chain, because every accepted step
    spends slack the next step is then judged against.
  - POST-HOC, no verdict: appetite dominated. The same filter read by a
    supervisor taking the motion with the most HEADROOM rather than the largest
    safe one loses 8 clips instead of 32 and completes 72 routes instead of 48 at
    E0. Carry it as exploratory. Do not promote it to a finding.

  THE DIRECT CONSEQUENCE FOR YOUR TASK: a five-clip route is a LONGER chain than
  anything measured. The v5 numbers do not cover it and must not be quoted as if
  they did. Your block is what extends them.

THE SHIPPED LAYER. src/assembly_recovery/cable_safety_filter_v4.py loads its
thresholds from the evidence record so a running check and a published number
cannot drift apart. It scores all three constraints and names the binding one,
maps the whole continuous action space in one call, and reports how much of each
constraint's headroom a move spends. It runs off a plain dict of what an
estimator says about its own error - use `declared_error(...)`. USE THIS LAYER.
Do not rewrite it and do not refit it.

TWO INSTRUMENT FINDINGS THAT CONSTRAIN EVERY CLAIM YOU MAKE:
  - The cable model DOES refine. The old refinement failure was a bending-damping
    scaling error in the control that tested it: a discrete bending joint carries
    the continuum damping as gamma*I/L, so halving the segment length must DOUBLE
    the per-joint damping. Corrected, a fourfold refinement settles at three
    integration rates and reproduces the registered settled geometry to 5.7 um.
    Minimum bend radius does NOT agree across refinements, so any curvature claim
    stays scoped to the 23-segment model at 4 kHz.
  - The routed cable is NOT at rest at the registered five-second settling
    deadline. It slides along the clip channel and comes to rest against the clip
    wall, 2.9 um past where the retention predicate's own lateral test sits. The
    deadline is part of the task definition, not an approximation to rest. DO NOT
    lengthen it and DO NOT change the retention predicate; either would change
    the task and void every comparison with the existing blocks.

TRAP 1 - A FUSE ONLY WELDS SOLIDS THAT INTERPENETRATE. Two boxes sharing a face
do not fuse: you get a compound of several solids and its tessellation is NOT
watertight. Overlap adjoining parts by about 0.01 mm. Measured and recorded:
face-touching gives 2 solids, a 0.01 mm overlap gives 1.

TRAP 2 - MUJOCO MOVES YOUR MESH, AND THAT IS FINE. The compiler recentres every
mesh on its centre of mass and rotates it to principal axes, so the raw vertex
data of a 30x18x3 base comes back with its 30 mm on Z. Do not fight it. The geom
frame compensates exactly: transform compiled vertices by `data.geom_xmat` and
`data.geom_xpos` and you recover CAD assembly coordinates. Measured worst
placement error over five parts: 0.000 mm. Export in ASSEMBLY coordinates and
give the geom no pos or quat.

TRAP 3 - THE CONTACT DEFAULTS ARE LOAD-BEARING, AND THIS ONE COST AN HOUR.
This project's scenes compile with

    <default><geom solref="0.004 1" solimp="0.95 0.99 0.001"/></default>

set at cable_constrained_v2.py:547. That is not cosmetic. Under MuJoCo's OWN
contact defaults a 0.23 g capsule dropped onto a 3 mm plate sinks through it and
from 8 mm up tunnels outright, at every timestep tried, with primitives just as
much as with meshes. Measured rest heights from an 8 / 20 / 50 mm drop: 1.996 mm
with the project defaults, and -73558 / -77048 / -77331 mm without them. ANY new
scene you build must inherit that defaults block.

TRAP 4 - COLLIDE AGAINST PRIMITIVES, NOT MESHES - BUT NOT FOR THE OBVIOUS REASON.
With the defaults above, mesh collision DOES hold the cable. It simply holds it
differently: 1.722 mm on one contact, against 1.996 mm on two for the primitive
channel. A quarter-millimetre is small in the abstract and large here, where the
retention predicate is decided within about 4 mm of channel half-width and the
settling behaviour was characterised to micrometres.

  THE RULE: CAD meshes are VISUAL geoms - contype="0" conaffinity="0" group="2".
  Collision stays on the primitive boxes and cylinders `build_fixture` already
  writes. The CAD makes it look like a real jig; the primitives make it behave
  like the one that was measured, bit-identical, so the CAD cannot invalidate a
  single published number. Say exactly this in the evidence record.

  (An earlier pass of this probe concluded mesh collision loses the cable
  outright. It was built without TRAP 3's defaults. Instrument, not collider.)

TRAP 5 - A CLIP NEEDS A FLOOR. The first probe drew two walls and two lips and
the cable fell out of the bottom. The compiled fixture has a shelf under its
clip, which is why it works. Any clip you author needs a base or a shelf.

A WORKING PATTERN, verified:
    import FreeCAD as App, Part, MeshPart
    doc = App.newDocument("cell")
    solid = Part.makeBox(...)            # assembly coordinates, millimetres
    fused = a.fuse(b).removeSplitter()   # only with ~0.01 mm interpenetration
    mesh = MeshPart.meshFromShape(Shape=solid, LinearDeflection=0.05,
                                  AngularDeflection=0.5, Relative=False)
    mesh.write("part.stl")               # assert mesh.isSolid()
    doc.saveAs("cell.FCStd")

5. THE BUILD - FOUR STAGES, EACH WITH A DONE-CONDITION ON DISK
=======================================================================
Everything measured so far is ONE connector and ONE recovery move. Real harness
work is routing: take a cable already attached at one end, lay it into a
sequence of clips and guides along a board, then seat the connector at the far
end. That is the task worth measuring, and it is exactly where a budget-aware
safety layer earns its place - which v5 turned from a slogan into a number.

STAGE 4 - THE ROUTING BLOCK            done-condition: artifacts/cell/block.json
------------------------------------------------------------------
One request is: from a settled free end, press the cable into clip 1, then clip
2, ... then seat the connector, with a declared dwell. Success is every required
clip retained AND the connector seated. This is a genuine EXTENSION of "held,
clip-preserving seating", not a relabelling - say so in the scope block of every
record you write.

At every routing step the supervisor asks the filter which moves are safe, issues
one, and the layer reports what it spent. Run at least three arms and make the
third earn its place: the filter taking the largest safe motion, the filter
taking the most headroom, and no filter at all. v5 makes the appetite comparison
a live question rather than a footnote - registering it here is the right way to
promote it.

Freeze the contract and COMMIT BEFORE LAUNCH. The margin must be at least twice
the coarsest metric resolution; the runner already refuses otherwise, and if it
refuses you add sequences, never lower the margin. Pilot 2 to 4 contexts, measure
the real per-request cost, then size the block to the time you actually have. A
three-motion sequence cost about 101,000 steps and 52 worker-seconds; a five-clip
route will cost more - measure, do not extrapolate.

Launch it in the background. If it will not finish inside this session, that is
FINE and expected: leave it running and say so in the handover you write at the
